Lost something in Korea? What to do, step by step

For travelers, students and residents · Free

Korea is good at returning lost items: wallets, phones and bags are routinely handed in to police stations, subway stations and lost-and-found centers. The hard part is finding where yours went — most of the information is in Korean, and items often turn up far from where you lost them.

1. Act quickly where you lost it

  • Subway or train: tell the staff at the nearest station office. Note the line, the direction, the time and roughly which car you were in.
  • Taxi: your card receipt shows the car details. If you booked through the Kakao T app, open the ride in your history to contact the driver or report a lost item.
  • Shop, café, hotel or venue: call or go back and ask — many places keep items for a while before handing them to the police.
  • Airport: ask the airport's lost-and-found service.

2. Search Korea's lost-and-found data — in your language

Items handed in to the police and many public offices are registered in Korea's national lost-and-found database (LOST112). It is in Korean only, and registration can take a few days after the item is found.

3. Need help with a Korean phone call?

Call 1330 — Korea Travel Helpline (24 hours). Interpreters can help you talk to the holding office. From a foreign phone number, dial +82-2-1330. In an emergency, dial 112 (police).

4. Picking it up

  • Call the holding office first to confirm they still have it and when you can come.
  • Bring your passport or ID — they will check you are the owner.
  • Be ready to describe the item (contents of a wallet, phone lock screen, case color) to prove it's yours.
  • Don't wait too long: under Korean law the owner's claim generally lasts about 6 months.

5. If you lost your passport

Report it to the police and contact your country's embassy or consulate in Korea for a replacement or an emergency travel document — keep searching in the meantime, as passports are often handed in.
